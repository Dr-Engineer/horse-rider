# Windhorse Rumble

A Road-Rash-style horse racing brawler set in Tibet in the Wood Horse Year (1954), told as an old rider's tall tale.
Speed wins races, and so does knocking everyone else off their horses.

**To play:** double-click `Play.command` on macOS, or `Play.bat` on Windows. It needs Godot 4.7. The first launch may ask you to allow it.

## The rules in brief

- Finish in the top 3 on all five courses to raise the Season. There are three Seasons.
- **Health** at zero crashes you, followed by a 7-second rest. Tap to shorten it.
- **Shake** builds with each hit's impact. Above 70 each new hit might unseat you, and at 100 you always fall, even at
  full health. Steering against the wobble calms you faster.
- **Wind trails:** a galloping horse leaves stiller air up to 55 m behind it, shown as turquoise streaks. Ride in one and your horse
  goes faster and tires less. That's how you catch whoever is ahead.
- **Ranged weapons** do far less damage and impact, and can never unseat a rider through impact alone. Nothing aims for you, and shots
  follow real physics: they fall under gravity and carry your horse's speed, so aim above a far target and ahead of a moving one.
- **Beside the road** stand bullseyes that pay by the ring, and flocks with wolves at them. Drive off a wolf for silver, but hit a sheep and you pay a fine.
- **Slam a rider from the side**, with a good deal more sideways momentum than they have, and you both carry on sideways. A strike with that edge, or right after a slam, does 1.5 times the damage.
- **Fists or a kick:** fists hit harder with no penalty. A kick lands late and slows your horse, but it shoves the target away.
- **Lungta** (the five flags) is your fortune. Pass mani walls and chortens with them on your right, cross high passes,
  parry and perform stunts to raise it.
- **Quiet Ground:** no fighting. It costs you fortune and silver.
- **Showing off pays:** near misses, knocked-off hats and knockdown streaks earn silver. Rivals may offer a side bet before the race.

## Voices

Riders talk during races in Tibetan and English, with subtitles, and the old rider tells the story aloud. Options sets the volume and language, and the
Phrasebook lets you hear each Tibetan phrase with its meaning.

The voices are generated by speech models, not recorded. English uses [Kokoro-82M](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M) (Apache 2.0). Tibetan uses
Meta's MMS-TTS ([Central](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-tts-bod), [Khams](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-tts-khg), [Amdo](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-tts-adx)),
under **CC BY-NC 4.0, so those lines are for non-commercial use only**. No Tibetan speaker has reviewed them yet: see `docs/VOICE_SCRIPT.md`.

## What's where

- `docs/`: design, style, world and cast, assets, architecture, research and its limits
- `docs/RESEARCH.md` summarises the research. The long-form notes stay on the original computer and aren't in this repo
- `art/`: scripts that generate every model, texture, font, sound and voice
- `assets/`: generated output, don't edit by hand
- `scripts/`, `scenes/`, `shaders/`, `data/`: the game
- `tests/`: `sim_balance` (headless rule checks), `aim_check` (shot physics and wind trails), `combat_check` (fists, kicks, slams), `roadside_check` (bullseyes, wolves, sheep), `road_check` (bends and clearances), `dressing_check` (nothing hovers or lands on the road), `steer_check` (how much steering a course asks for), `autoplay` (bot plays with rendering), `lookdev` and `hazard_view` (art review), `mix_check` (loudness)
- `tools/run_hidden.ps1`: runs Godot on an invisible desktop on Windows, so tests never take focus
- `tools/record_demo.sh`: records the demo video from real gameplay

## Setting up on another computer

Ask Claude Code on that computer to clone this repo and follow `docs/NEW_COMPUTER_SETUP.md`. `docs/HANDOFF.md` says where
the project stands. Models, textures and sounds are in Git LFS, so a clone without it gets placeholder files.

There are no `.blend` files. Every model is generated by the scripts in `art/blender/`, and the results are in
`assets/models/` as `.glb`, which Blender can open directly.

## Not yet done

- A Tibetan reader should review the spoken phrases, names, dress, story and the dob-dob rival before any public release.
- The Tibetan voices must be replaced before any paid release, because of their non-commercial licence.
- The balance comes from bots, not playtesters. The sound and voices have been measured but not yet judged by ear. Gamepad is untested.
- The Visitors (Mongol riders, cowboys, British, fantasy horse lords) are designed in `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` but not built.
